
INSURANCE CLAIM INVESTIGATION GUIDELINES

Document ID: GUIDE-001
Version: 1.0
Purpose: Synthetic insurance investigation demonstration.

SECTION 1: RISK ASSESSMENT

Predictive model scores are decision-support indicators.
A high risk score does not establish fraudulent activity.
Uncalibrated model scores must not be interpreted as
actual probabilities of fraud.

SECTION 2: RECENTLY PURCHASED POLICIES

Claims submitted within 90 days of policy inception
may warrant additional verification.

Recommended actions:
- Verify policy effective dates.
- Review coverage details.
- Confirm the date of the reported incident.

SECTION 3: MULTIPLE PREVIOUS CLAIMS

Multiple previous claims may justify additional review.
However, frequent claims can have legitimate explanations.

Recommended actions:
- Review available claim history.
- Verify the circumstances of previous incidents.
- Identify potential inconsistencies.

SECTION 4: HIGH CLAIM-TO-VALUE RATIO

Claims exceeding 80 percent of vehicle value may
require additional financial verification.

Recommended actions:
- Verify vehicle valuation.
- Review independent repair estimates.
- Confirm the extent of reported damage.

SECTION 5: DELAYED CLAIM REPORTING

Reporting delays exceeding 10 days may warrant
additional investigation.

Recommended actions:
- Request an explanation for the reporting delay.
- Verify the incident timeline.
- Review available supporting documentation.

SECTION 6: MISSING SUPPORTING DOCUMENTATION

Missing police reports or witnesses do not establish
fraudulent activity.

Recommended actions:
- Determine whether documentation is applicable.
- Request available supporting evidence.
- Consider legitimate explanations for missing records.

SECTION 7: REPAIR COST DISCREPANCIES

Material differences between claimed amounts and
repair estimates may require financial verification.

Recommended actions:
- Obtain itemized repair estimates.
- Review applicable coverage and additional expenses.
- Verify claimed damages.

SECTION 8: ANOMALY DETECTION

Statistically unusual claims are not necessarily fraudulent.
Anomaly detection should complement supervised
predictive models and human judgment.

SECTION 9: HUMAN REVIEW

Elevated risk scores, unusual patterns, and unresolved
inconsistencies may trigger human investigation.

No claim should be automatically denied based solely
on predictive models, anomaly detection, business rules,
or AI-generated recommendations.

SECTION 10: RESPONSIBLE AI

All investigation recommendations must be evidence-based.
AI systems must not fabricate claim details or evidence.
Uncertainty and missing information must be disclosed.
Relevant knowledge base sources should be cited.

These guidelines are fictional and intended exclusively
for a synthetic data science portfolio demonstration.
